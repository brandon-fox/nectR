# **Non-Dilutive Funding, Governance, and University Pilot Strategy for nectR**

As of November 2026, nectR is well-positioned to pursue non-dilutive academic and public-goods backing because its architecture—an MIT-licensed, zero-fee, closed-loop peer tutoring economy (RAW\_POLLEN university sponsorship converting into LIQUID\_NECTR tutor credits and autonomous daughter-Hive swarming)—aligns with a decisive 2025–2026 shift among grantmakers away from speculative token launches toward provable mechanism design, open-source security, and measurable institutional outcomes1. Converting nectR’s whitepaper (paper/, docs/theory/), multi-agent simulations (experiments/), and institutional proposals (business/, docs/business/) into funded university pilots requires navigating three structural realities: several high-profile open-source and education grant programs are paused or concluded entering 20275, campus IT offices require complete abstraction of blockchain keys behind FERPA-compliant Single Sign-On and LTI 1.3 standards8, and LIQUID\_NECTR redemption mechanics must be legally structured to avoid IRS wage-tax reporting and federal Title IV financial aid overaward penalties11.  
This advisory assessment maps the top 15 active funding and backing programs across academic and non-speculative Web3 ecosystems, evaluates how nectR’s MIT repository and simulation telemetry affect peer-review competitiveness, analyzes university Technology Transfer Office (TTO), privacy, tax, and IT procurement gates alongside multi-campus OSS governance options, reviews transferable empirical precedents, and lays out a staged 24-month roadmap to a multi-campus pilot.

## **How MIT Code and Empirical Simulations Affect Grant Competitiveness**

Across federal computing, education, and Web3 panels, nectR’s existing repository—uniting formal proofs (paper/, docs/theory/), multi-agent simulation telemetry (experiments/), and MIT-licensed software—provides a strong competitive edge only when calibrated to each reviewer cohort rather than pitched as finished commercial software.  
In **NSF Future CoRe (CISE)** and **PESOSE** reviews, panels typically consist of 15–20 computer scientists in which only 2–3 are domain specialists and the remainder are generalists who follow the specialists’ lead66. Proposals routinely fail from "contribution confusion" when reviewers cannot tell whether the primary scientific contribution is a theoretical complexity/equilibrium bound, a software system, or an evaluation methodology66. nectR should write its introduction for CS generalists—framing peer-tutoring liquidity as a distributed coordination problem—while placing its *Apis mellifera* game-theoretic equilibrium proofs in the technical core for the 2–3 mechanism-design experts66. Equally important, NSF’s April 2026 Data Management & Sharing Plan mandate and open-science review norms penalize vague promises to release code "upon request"; pointing directly to an active MIT-licensed GitHub repository with FAIR-compliant simulation scripts and telemetry outputs strengthens both **Intellectual Merit** (reproducibility) and **Broader Impacts** (shared research infrastructure)17.  
For **behavioral economics and EdTech funders** (NSF IUSE, DRMS, and Russell Sage Foundation), multi-agent simulation data does *not* substitute for human-subjects causal evidence on student retention. Instead, experiments/ telemetry serves as vital preliminary mechanistic validation proving that the RAW\_POLLEN to LIQUID\_NECTR loop resists hoarding, Sybil gaming, and velocity collapse before exposing undergraduates to a live Randomized Controlled Trial (RCT) or quasi-experimental campus pilot33. Note also that RSF lists its older "Behavioral Economics Small Grants" under completed programs, having shifted to **Behavioral Science and Decision Making in Context** (Trustee/Core and Presidential tiers) and **Pipeline Grants** (which partner with J-PAL North America for pilot RCTs)33.  
Among **DPG and Web3 foundations**, evaluation criteria diverge sharply. **Ethereum Foundation ESP** requires permissive open-source/open-access licensing and alignment with Ethereum-relevant problems (including institutional economics, mechanism design, and privacy), making nectR’s theory and simulation work eligible for Wishlist/RFP grants, the Academic Grants Round, or a PhD Fellowship without launching a mainnet token3. By contrast, the **Sovereign Tech Agency** selects projects on six criteria led by **prevalence**—meaning nectR will not qualify for Sovereign Tech Fund commissioning until multiple campuses or external EdTech tools actively depend on its protocol15. Finally, chain-tied ecosystem grants (**Stellar SCF**, **Algorand**, **NEAR**) should remain secondary targets: Stellar’s Research Awards fund chain-agnostic cryptography/distributed-systems scholarship54, whereas SCF, Algorand, and NEAR grants mandate ecosystem-specific deployment57, which can complicate university IT procurement if tied to public-chain gas or token volatility.

## **University Procurement, Compliance, and OSS Governance**

Securing university sponsorship (RAW\_POLLEN funding) requires clearing four institutional compliance gates—Tech Transfer, Privacy/IRB and Bursar/Financial Aid, Campus IT Security, and Multi-Campus OSS Governance—before a single student enrolls in a pilot.

### **Tech Transfer Office (TTO) and Inbound IP Posture (MIT \+ DCO vs. CLA)**

University TTOs evaluate open-source software through a sharp legal split between *copyright* and *patentable inventions*71. Most TTOs approve permissive copyright release under the **MIT License** when no exclusive commercial licensee is viable, provided there are no conflicting sponsor obligations or incompatible copyleft dependencies71. However, under institutional policies modeled on MIT Policy 13.1, the university claims ownership over IP created using university funds, graduate research assistantships (where graduate students are treated as employees), or federally funded capstones and UROPs for credit, while self-directed student coursework created without sponsored funds or institutional resources generally remains student-owned73. If federal grant funds supported any component, Bayh-Dole and 2 CFR 200.315 rules require formal invention disclosure and a patentability check *before* public code release71. Note that while the MIT License minimizes adoption friction, it contains no explicit patent grant (unlike Apache 2.0), so TTOs will verify whether any collaborator intends to patent underlying consensus algorithms prior to MIT release71.  
For external multi-campus contributors, nectR must choose between a **Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO)** and a **Contributor License Agreement (CLA)**. A **DCO (v1.1)** requires a per-commit Signed-off-by certification (automated via GitHub PR checks and optionally backed by GnuPG commit signatures) confirming the contributor has the right to submit code under the project’s MIT License76. While a DCO does not contain an express license grant or allow the lead maintainer to unilaterally switch to a proprietary dual-license later76, it avoids requiring every participating university’s legal counsel to negotiate and continually update a Corporate CLA (CCLA) Schedule A as students and postdocs rotate off campus—an administrative barrier that led GitLab to drop its CLA in favor of a DCO77. Adopting **MIT \+ DCO 1.1** is therefore the optimal low-friction posture for federated academic development.

### **FERPA, GDPR, and Student Financial Aid / Tax Architecture**

Student-facing campus ledgers face immediate rejection if they overlook educational privacy, immutability law, or tax/financial-aid rules. Under **FERPA**, student names, campus IDs, course enrollments, and tutoring attendance records are protected education records; even university employees with operational access cannot pass student data to faculty researchers without explicit Registrar authorization and IRB review82. Under **GDPR**, European Data Protection Board (EDPB) guidelines emphasize that blockchain immutability clashes with the **right to rectification** and **right to erasure**, and regulatory guidance notes that pseudonymous public-key wallet addresses constitute personal data if transactions can be linked back to a natural person8. nectR must enforce a **hybrid off-chain PII / on-chain proof architecture**: all student identities, tutor certifications, and session logs reside inside a campus-controlled relational database (supporting deletion and correction), while the ledger records only salted/peppered one-way hashes or Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) validating session completion without exposing PII8. Any EU campus pilot also requires a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)84.  
Equally critical, campus Bursars and Financial Aid Offices enforce strict IRS and federal **Title IV** boundaries on student incentives. Under IRS Publication 970 and university payroll rules, cash equivalents (including gift cards) and student achievement awards are taxable income requiring W-2 or 1099-NEC reporting, and scholarships are tax-exempt *only* when restricted to qualified tuition/course expenses and *not* representing payment for services (outside narrow statutory work-college or service-scholarship exceptions)11. Furthermore, non-exempt financial awards must be reported to the Office of Student Financial Aid (OSFAS) to prevent Title IV overawards12. Consequently, LIQUID\_NECTR earned by tutoring cannot be silently redeemed for bookstore gift cards, cash, or service-linked tuition waivers without triggering tax withholding and Title IV recalculations. Early pilots should restrict LIQUID\_NECTR to a **closed-loop non-monetary service-time credit** (exchangeable 1:1 for peer tutoring hours or non-monetary academic badges) or route any university-sponsored RAW\_POLLEN stipends directly through standard campus payroll and financial-aid systems11.

### **Campus IT Security and EdTech Integration Standards**

University Chief Information Security Officers (CISOs) require three technical and compliance deliverables prior to approving a campus pilot. First is **HECVAT 4 (v4.1.6)**, released by EDUCAUSE in February 2025, which consolidated the legacy Full, Lite, and On-Premise spreadsheets into one triage-driven Excel workbook (321 total questions across 7 sections, including \~61 asterisked "Core 60" baseline questions for low-risk pilots plus new **AI/Machine Learning** and **Chief Privacy Officer GDPR/residency** tabs)89. Although HECVAT is a self-assessment rather than a formal certification or FERPA substitute, completing it is a de facto higher-education procurement gate90. Second is **1EdTech LTI 1.3 and LTI Advantage**: institutions in 2026 routinely reject deprecated LTI 1.1 tools, expecting OIDC/JWT-based LTI 1.3 launches paired with **Names and Role Provisioning Services (NRPS)** for scoped course rosters and **Deep Linking 2.0** inside Canvas, Moodle, or Blackboard9. Third is **SAML 2.0 / InCommon Shibboleth SSO** paired with automated term-rollover deprovisioning (SCIM or SIS enrollment sync)9, ensuring students authenticate with existing campus credentials and never handle cryptographic seed phrases.

## **Case Studies: Whitepaper and Simulation to Institutional Pilots**

A targeted search across academic and open-source records found **no publicly documented case study of a university-born blockchain peer-tutoring token system** that has progressed from multi-agent simulation to a live multi-campus pilot. Rather than extrapolating from unverified projects, nectR should draw directly on four documented empirical precedents across mutual credit, campus timebanking, legal service-credit framing, and higher-education OSS:

> 1. **Grassroots Economics / Sarafu Network (Kenya, 2011–2024+):** Though NGO- rather than university-born, Sarafu is the closest operational precedent for nectR’s mechanism design. Originating as paper vouchers (2011), moving to a permissioned POA chain (2018), open-source Ethereum/xDai (2020), and Celo’s Commitment Pooling Protocol (2023), the network reached 50,000–52,000 households (\~\$3 million in annual trade in its prior-year peak) and, as of September 2024, supported 33 commitment pools, 239 Community Asset Vouchers, and 3,149 monthly active users104. Three tactical lessons apply directly to nectR: the team translated mechanism theory into practice using physical **bean simulation games** before digital launch105; users transacted via **USSD/SMS text menus** without knowing a blockchain existed underneath104; and donor seeding through local anchor hubs (including schools) combined with a **2% monthly demurrage fee** prevented hoarding and drove circulation velocity106—providing an empirical calibration point for nectR’s RAW\_POLLEN decay parameters.  
> 2. **Dharma Drum Buddhist College Time-Banking Pilot (Taiwan, 2009):** One of the few peer-reviewed university time-banking implementations enabled student volunteers to earn time credits redeemable for institutional tuition waivers108. It demonstrates that campus mutual-credit economies gain student traction when backed by predictable institutional value rather than external market pricing, while underscoring why U.S. deployments must isolate service credits from Title IV and IRS scholarship-for-services rules11.  
> 3. **Japan’s Nationwide Time-Credit Legal Clearing Model:** Cross-regional Japanese time-credit networks avoid wage-and-money-transmitter classification by denominating credits strictly in equal service duration (never priced by market labor intensity), prohibiting free speculative circulation, and structuring participation as paired "gratuitous mandate" and "remunerated mandate" clearing contracts with the central organizer88. Adopting this organizer-cleared service-duration framing gives university counsel a clear non-monetary compliance perimeter for LIQUID\_NECTR.  
> 4. **Sakai / Jasig to Apereo Foundation & Sloan Academic OSPOs:** Higher education seeded foundational OSS such as Apache, Linux, R, and Python, yet campus-built EdTech platforms frequently stalled after initial research grants expired97. In 2012, the university-born Sakai and Jasig software communities merged into the **Apereo Foundation** to establish neutral multi-institution governance and sustainable vendor support100. Combined with Alfred P. Sloan Foundation and Chan Zuckerberg Initiative investments in university Open Source Program Offices (OSPOs, including the University of California system), this trajectory confirms that academic OSS scales across campuses only when R\&D consortiums graduate into dedicated higher-ed foundation stewardship30.
